HOLI FESTIVAL

About Holi

The festival is traditionally spread across two days, starting with Holika Dahan on the eve of Holi. On this night, grand community bonfires are lit to re-enact the mythological victory of the young devotee Prahlad over his evil aunt Holika, symbolizing how pure faith conquers destructive malice. The following morning, known as Rangwali Holi or Dhuleti, the atmosphere transforms into a joyous, free-for-all carnival of joy.During the main day of celebrations, streets and neighborhoods across India turn into living canvases. People of all ages and backgrounds gather to smear each other with brightly colored powders (gulal) and splash tinted water using water guns and balloons. Amid the laughter and playful chaos, people dance to rhythmic folk beats and indulge in traditional festive delicacies like sweet Gujiyas and refreshing milk-based Thandai. Beyond its playful nature, Holi carries deep social significance. It acts as a powerful equalizer that dissolves societal barriers of age and status, prompting people to forgive past grievances, repair strained relationships, and embrace the spirit of universal brotherhood.

Main Celebrations

  • Playing with colours
  • Traditional music
  • Dancing
  • Preparing traditional food
  • Meeting family and friends
