DIWALI FESTIVAL

About Diwali

Diwali, the "Festival of Lights," is one of India's most significant and widely celebrated festivals, symbolizing the spiritual triumph of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance. Derived from the Sanskrit word Deepavali, meaning a "row of lighted lamps," this five-day festival unites people across different cultures and religions in a grand celebration of hope and renewal.The festival is deeply rooted in rich mythology. For many, it marks the joyous return of Lord Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana to their kingdom of Ayodhya after 14 years of exile and their victory over the demon king Ravana. To illuminate their path on a dark, new moon night, the citizens of Ayodhya lit thousands of earthen lamps (diyas). Across other traditions, Diwali honors Goddess Lakshmi (the deity of wealth and prosperity), commemorates Lord Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura, and marks Lord Mahavira's liberation in Jainism and the celebration of Bandi Chhor Divas in Sikhism.Celebrations are marked by vibrant rituals, beginning with cleaning and decorating homes with intricate Rangoli designs and glowing diyas. Families dress in new clothes, exchange sweets and gifts, and gather for the auspicious Lakshmi Puja to pray for prosperity and health. Ultimately, Diwali transcends religious boundaries, spreading a universal message of peace, togetherness, and the enduring power of goodwill.

Main Celebrations

  • Lighting lamps
  • Decorating homes
  • Making Rangoli
  • Exchanging sweets and gifts
  • Family celebrations
