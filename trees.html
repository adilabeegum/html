Trees are perennial woody plants characterized by a single main stem or trunk that grows to a considerable height and supports elevated branches and leaves. As the longest-living organisms on Earth, they form the backbone of terrestrial ecosystems by providing critical habitats, stabilizing soil, and generating a massive portion of the world's oxygen through photosynthesis. They are divided into two main categories: deciduous trees, which shed their leaves annually, and evergreen trees, which retain their foliage year-round. Beyond their ecological value, trees are an indispensable resource for humans, offering shade, regulating local climates, and supplying essential materials like timber, fuel, fruits, and life-saving medicinal compounds.
